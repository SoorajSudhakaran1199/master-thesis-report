\section{Conclusion}
\label{ch7_sec:conclusion}
 
This thesis presented the development and evaluation of an automated robotic system for press brake sheet metal bending. The objective was to reduce manual programming effort while improving adaptability and execution reliability in industrial bending operations.
 
The analysis of the state of the art in Chapter~\ref{ch:state-of-art} identified key limitations in existing approaches, including a strong dependence on manual programming, limited adaptability to changing workpiece conditions, and a lack of integrated planning frameworks. Based on these observations, a set of functional and non-functional requirements was defined in Chapter~\ref{ch:system-concept-methodology}, focusing on automation, flexibility, and system robustness.
 
In Chapter~\ref{ch:development-concepts}, multiple solution concepts were evaluated, leading to the selection of Concept~B as the most suitable approach. This concept introduced a structured planning pipeline that integrates orientation determination, grip planning, regrip handling, and trajectory generation into a unified system.
 
Chapter~\ref{ch_5:implementation} described the complete implementation of the selected concept. The system was realised as a modular software architecture with clearly defined layers, enabling separation of concerns and maintainability. Key functionalities such as constraint-based grip planning, automatic regrip determination, and structured trajectory generation were implemented to support fully automated operation.
 
The evaluation presented in Chapter~\ref{ch_6:evaluation} demonstrated that the system successfully fulfils the defined requirements. Functional validation confirmed that each component of the planning pipeline operates correctly under varying conditions. System-level evaluation showed that the integrated solution produces consistent and feasible robot programs without manual intervention.
 
A comparison with manual programming approaches highlighted a significant reduction in programming effort and adaptation time. Tasks that traditionally require manual adjustment and reprogramming can be handled automatically by the system, enabling faster and more consistent operation. This demonstrates that the primary objective of reducing operator dependency and improving efficiency has been achieved.
 
Overall, the developed system provides a systematic and adaptable solution for robotic press brake bending. By combining automated decision-making with structured validation, the approach addresses key limitations of existing methods and contributes to the advancement of flexible robotic manufacturing systems.
 
\section{Contributions}
\label{ch7_sec:contributions}
 
The main contributions of this thesis can be summarised as follows:
 
\begin{itemize}
\item Development of a fully automated planning pipeline for press brake bending, integrating orientation determination, grip planning, regrip handling, and trajectory generation
 
\item Introduction of a constraint-based grip planning method that ensures both geometric feasibility and sufficient holding force
 
\item Design of an adaptive regrip strategy that determines required reorientation dynamically without predefined sequences
 
\item Implementation of a structured trajectory generation approach that ensures smooth and feasible robot motion
 
\item Integration of validation mechanisms for safety and feasibility, including pre-execution checks and runtime monitoring
 
\item Demonstration of reduced programming effort and improved adaptability through comparison with manual programming approaches
\end{itemize}
 
These contributions collectively address the challenges identified in the state of the art and provide a foundation for further development of automated robotic bending systems.
 
\section{Limitations}
\label{ch7_sec:limitations}
 
Despite the successful implementation and evaluation of the system, several limitations remain.
 
The evaluation was conducted entirely within a virtual simulation environment. While this enables controlled testing, it does not fully capture real-world factors such as sensor noise, calibration inaccuracies, and mechanical tolerances.
 
In addition, the system does not include comprehensive collision detection or advanced dynamic modelling of contact interactions. These aspects are important for ensuring safe operation in more complex or constrained environments.
 
Furthermore, the current implementation focuses on a specific class of sheet metal geometries and bending operations. More complex geometries or multi-step industrial processes may require additional extensions to the planning pipeline.
 
\section{Future Work}
\label{ch7_sec:future_work}
 
Based on the identified limitations, several directions for future work can be proposed.
 
Although the developed system achieves a functional and structured approach to robotic sheet handling and bending, it is inherently constrained by a series of simplifying assumptions introduced during the design phase. These constraints, while beneficial for ensuring stability and predictability, limit the system’s applicability to more complex and realistic industrial scenarios. Consequently, several avenues for future work emerge, aimed at improving flexibility, intelligence, and robustness.

A key limitation of the current system lies in its strict assumption of rectangular sheet geometries aligned with the global coordinate frame. In practical manufacturing environments, components often exhibit irregular shapes and arbitrary orientations. Future work should therefore focus on generalizing the geometric representation to support non-rectangular profiles, including polygonal and free-form geometries. Additionally, enabling the system to operate independently of fixed axis alignment would significantly enhance its adaptability. This would require a more advanced geometric abstraction layer capable of handling arbitrary edge definitions and diverse feature types, including non-circular cutouts.

The bending capabilities of the system also present a significant opportunity for advancement. The restriction to single, 90-degree bends per edge simplifies the planning process but does not reflect the complexity of real-world fabrication tasks. Future developments should introduce support for variable bend angles and multiple bends along a single edge. More importantly, this necessitates the formulation of an intelligent bend sequence planning framework capable of optimizing operation order, minimizing regrip requirements, and ensuring process feasibility. Such a framework could leverage constraint-based optimization or heuristic search methods to generate efficient and manufacturable bending strategies.

From a robotics perspective, motion planning remains an area with considerable scope for improvement. The current system demonstrates reduced efficiency when handling certain orientations, particularly those involving significant rotational transformations. Future work should integrate advanced motion planning techniques that ensure collision-free, time-optimal trajectories. Furthermore, introducing adaptive calibration mechanisms would allow the system to remain functional even when the robot base or surrounding fixtures are repositioned, thereby reducing dependency on manual reconfiguration. The incorporation of learning-based methods could further enhance performance by enabling the system to refine its motion strategies through repeated execution.

The gripping subsystem, currently limited to vacuum-based handling, represents another constraint on system versatility. While effective for flat and uniform surfaces, vacuum gripping is not universally applicable. Expanding the system to support multiple gripping modalities, including mechanical and magnetic solutions, would broaden its operational scope. In addition, the integration of sensor feedback, such as force or slip detection, would enable real-time validation of grasp stability and improve overall reliability.

Regrip operations in the present implementation are restricted to discrete rotational increments, which limits the flexibility of part orientation. Future work should explore continuous orientation control and develop more sophisticated regrip planning strategies aimed at minimizing intermediate handling steps. Streamlining opposite-side operations into a unified workflow, rather than relying on separate scripts, would further improve system efficiency and maintainability.

Safety considerations represent a critical gap in the current system. The absence of collision detection and trajectory validation poses a significant limitation for deployment in real-world environments. Future enhancements should prioritize the integration of real-time collision avoidance mechanisms and safe motion planning algorithms. Expanding the scope of the safety monitoring system to account for dynamic interactions between the robot, workpieces, and surrounding infrastructure would also be essential.

Finally, the system’s reliance on fixed object identifiers and predefined configurations reduces its robustness in dynamic environments. Future work should investigate the integration of perception-driven approaches, such as vision-based object recognition and tracking, to enable more flexible and autonomous operation. Additionally, incorporating error detection and recovery strategies would allow the system to handle unexpected deviations more effectively, thereby improving overall resilience.

In conclusion, future developments should aim to transition the system from a rigid, rule-based framework toward a more adaptive and intelligent architecture. By addressing limitations in geometry handling, bending flexibility, motion planning, gripping strategies, regrip operations, safety, and system adaptability, the proposed solution can evolve into a more comprehensive and industrially viable robotic automation platform.
 
\section{Final Remarks}
\label{ch7_sec:final_remarks}
 
This thesis demonstrates that automated planning and execution of robotic press brake bending is both feasible and beneficial. By reducing manual programming effort and improving adaptability, the developed system contributes to more efficient and flexible manufacturing processes.
 
The presented approach provides a structured foundation for future research and development in robotic bending and related automation tasks.
